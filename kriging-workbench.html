Kriging workbench

Five kriging methods, one real dataset. 155 soil samples and a 3103-cell prediction grid from the Meuse river floodplain (Stein, NL) — the classic teaching dataset from Burrough & McDonnell, distributed with R's sp/gstat packages. Heavy-metal concentrations are modeled in log10 space (they are strongly right-skewed). Work through the tabs left to right: pick a variable, look at it, shape a variogram, krige it, then turn the result into an exceedance-probability map.
Up to date.

Variable

Pick 1–3 secondary metals (always known at all 155 sites) to help predict the sparsely-sampled primary metal. With more than one, this becomes a true multivariate cokriging under a linear model of coregionalization: every variable pair shares the same spatial correlation shape, scaled by the sill and the pairwise correlation actually measured in the data.
Fraction of the 155 sites where the (expensive) primary metal is "known" in this demo; the co-variables are always known everywhere. At 100% coverage, cokriging has nothing left to add over ordinary kriging — a real, textbook result for co-located sampling. Only used by the CK method (set on the Kriging tab).
Method (SK / OK / UK / RK / CK) is chosen on the Kriging tab — it acts on whichever variable(s) you pick here.
